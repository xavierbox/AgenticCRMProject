### Further Investigations to Rule Out Channeling
Given the constraints of having only rates, well locations, and possibly pressure at the producers, here are some steps to investigate channeling:

1. **Tracer Tests**:
   - If feasible, conduct inter-well tracer tests to directly measure the connectivity and flow paths between injectors and producers. This is the most direct way to confirm channeling.

2. **Pressure Transient Analysis**:
   - Analyze pressure responses at producers during injection rate changes at nearby injectors. A rapid pressure response at a producer following an injection change at a specific injector could indicate a direct connection or channeling.

3. **Cross-Correlation Analysis**:
   - Perform cross-correlation analysis between injection and production rates. A high correlation with minimal time lag could indicate a strong and fast connection, potentially due to channeling.

4. **Geological and Reservoir Mapping**:
   - Use well logs, seismic data, and geological models to identify high-permeability zones or fractures that could act as preferential flow paths.

5. **Dynamic Simulation**:
   - If possible, use a full-field reservoir simulation model to validate the CRM results and investigate the flow dynamics in more detail.

6. **Step-Rate Testing**:
   - Conduct step-rate tests on injectors to observe the pressure response and assess the presence of high-permeability pathways.

7. **Water Cut Trends**:
   - Monitor water cut trends at producers. A sudden increase in water cut following an increase in injection rate could indicate channeling.

8. **Comparison with Historical Data**:
   - Compare current CRM results with historical data to identify changes in connectivity or response times that might indicate the development of new flow paths.
